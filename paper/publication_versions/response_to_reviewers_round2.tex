\section*{Scope}
This letter answers a pre-submission review of two manuscripts by a panel of seven
referees: sequential statistics, statistical disclosure control, differential privacy,
central bank statistics, machine learning, reproducibility, and a general referee who
took the part of Reviewer~2. The manuscripts are
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Adaptive certification of attack-conditional disclosure risk across release
programmes}, with its supplement, for the Journal of Privacy and Confidentiality
(the article); and
\item \emph{Governing privacy risk across repeated statistical releases: probability
models, programme tolerances and accountable decisions}, a long-form author manuscript
for IFC Working Paper consideration (the long form).
\end{itemize}
Titles changed in revision; the first-round reports use the old ones. The full reports,
including a second-round check by Reviewer~2, are in \texttt{docs/panel\_review/} of the
repository.

\section*{Summary of changes}
Four changes alter what the manuscripts claim.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The pooled credit-register excess is split into its two causes.} In the
original attack the pooled adversary held one exposure reading per vintage, so it held
six times the auxiliary information of the single-release adversary. A new table
(long form, Table~12; article, Table~3) changes one factor
at a time on the same targets. With no suppression the rate is 0.079 for one release
and one reading and 0.209 for the full pooled attack. Extra readings alone give 0.132;
pooling the six vintages with one reading gives 0.136, with an upper bound of 0.145,
above the tolerance of 0.12. The programme finding survives, but both manuscripts now
attribute only part of the gap to pooling.
\item \textbf{The cell-count floor is chosen by the upper bound, on a finer grid.} The
old headline chose $k_{\min}=50$ by point estimate, and 50 was the top of the grid. The
revised run adds a fine grid (20 to 100) and applies the exact upper bound (long form, Table~13). The smallest
tested floor that meets the tolerance is now 40, which keeps 23,068 loans against 32,581
with no floor.
\item \textbf{Noisy counts are composed across vintages.} The pooled column for the
noisy-count policies had repeated the single-release value. Each vintage now draws fresh
noise and the adversary combines the readings, so the programme spends six times the
stated $\varepsilon$. The count rows measure class uniqueness, not loan identification,
and both manuscripts now say they cannot be compared with the loan tolerance.
\item \textbf{The article runs its gate on a disclosure problem.} A new experiment
(\texttt{scripts/adaptive\_audit\_credit.py}, article Table~2) registers a
single-release and a pooled coordinate for five policies on the synthetic credit
register, over 20 seeds, and compares the gate with a frozen fixed-sample family at the
same maximum budget. Every policy passes its single-release coordinate. Only $k=50$
passes the pooled coordinate with high probability (19 of 20 seeds). At equal budget the
frozen family certifies more often; the gate stops earlier.
\end{enumerate}
The code changes behind these results, and the repository fixes, are listed at the end.

\section*{Reviewer 2}

\point{R2.1 (major).}{Theorem 1 is online alpha-spending over test supermartingales, and
the relevant literature is not cited.}
\reply Agreed. The article has a new related-work section (Section~2) that cites
alpha-investing (Foster and Stine 2008), online familywise error control (Tian and
Ramdas 2021, Statistical Methods in Medical Research), test martingales and e-values
(Shafer et al.\ 2011; Vovk and Wang 2021; Ramdas et al.\ 2023), betting confidence
sequences (Howard et al.\ 2021; Waudby-Smith and Ramdas 2024) and Ville (1939). A remark
after the theorem states the reduction to online Bonferroni and names what the article
adds: the coordinate definition, the attack-sampling condition, the pooled-coordinate
requirement, the audit-cost rule and the measured application.

\point{R2.2 (major).}{The paper hands the novelty judgement to the referee.}
\reply Every such sentence is deleted, including those on priority, ``the journal
mathematics'' and the author's status. The abstract ends with a statement of what the
gate requires and costs.

\point{R2.3 (major).}{Proposition 1 is an intersection attack; Ganta et al.\ is not
cited; the construction needs a shared identifier; $q$ has three meanings.}
\reply The article states a formal threat model (Assumption~1) in which each release
carries a pseudonym constant across vintages and the adversary knows the target's full
quasi-identifier vector. The proposition uses the long form's construction, with $s$
for the alphabet size, and is described as a standard non-composition example that
fixes the estimand, citing Sweeney (2002), Wang and Fung (2006), Ganta, Kasiviswanathan
and Smith (2008) and Dinur and Nissim (2003). The long form now states the same
pseudonym condition in its proposition and in the credit-register threat model, and
notes that a rotating pseudonym would make the pooled figures overstate the risk.

\point{R2.4 (major).}{The gate is never run on a disclosure problem.}
\reply Partly addressed. The new credit-register experiment above runs the gate with
adaptive registration and compares it with a frozen family at equal budget. Not done: a
run on the public Census file, comparison with the beta-binomial confidence sequence and
with an adaptive-bet e-process, and a case with reused targets. The article says so. The
experiment also does not exercise adaptive registration in a way a frozen family could
not match, because every single-release coordinate certified in every seed; the article
states this.

\point{R2.5 (major).}{The overlap with the long form is not disclosed in the
manuscript.}
\reply The long form is cited as a reference. A first-page footnote names the results
reproduced from it and the results that are new (the theorem, the weights and cost
propositions, the toy audit and the credit-register audit). The carried-over tables are
cut to the decomposition table the argument needs, and the supplement repeats the
statement. The cover letter will repeat it again and mention the Lisbon presentation.

\point{R2.6 (major).}{The neural section does not belong in the article.}
\reply The main text now has one paragraph: any predictor may supply the bet if it is
fixed before the outcome is seen, and validity does not depend on its accuracy. The
network, its gradient, the Lipschitz bound (now with the input-standardisation factor)
and the benchmark are in the supplement. The benchmark was rerun to convergence over ten
seeds on a third held-out set: logistic regression has test log loss 0.2556 and the
three-layer network 0.2576, so the old claim that depth helps is withdrawn.

\point{R2.7 (major).}{Replacing $\alpha$ by $\alpha/q$ after inspection is invalid.}
\reply Corrected in the long form: $\alpha/q$ applies to a family fixed before any
outcome is seen; hypotheses added after inspection need a registered spending sequence,
as in the article.

\point{R2.8 (major).}{The long form is too long and lists ten contributions.}
\reply Partly addressed. The contribution list has four items, the unsupported and
overstated sentences are reworded, the four non-central-bank case studies and the neural
material are in appendices, and a reader's guide sends a central bank reader to the
governance rule, the credit evaluation and the protocol. The full reordering was not
done. The paper is 74 pages, because the decision record, two new tables and the added
caveats outweigh what moved.

\point{R2.9 (major).}{The article is not organised as a journal article.}
\reply It now runs: introduction with contributions, related work, setting and threat
model, gate and theory, experiments, discussion and limitations, conclusion. Terms are
defined before use.

\point{R2.10 (minor).}{References are thin or incomplete.}
\reply The article's list grew from 8 to 56 entries and the supplement now has its own list of 16; the long form grew from 61 to 109. Each added work is cited where it supports a sentence, and each entry was checked against its Crossref, OpenAlex or publisher record. Bates et al.\ is cited as
J.\ ACM 68(6); Xu et al.\ as NeurIPS 2024; Fellegi--Sunter is no longer cited for
attacker models (Duncan and Lambert 1989 is). Zhou et al.\ (arXiv:2605.07002, May 2026)
was checked on arXiv by the differential privacy referee and is kept. Still open:
Elliot and Dale (1999) is left out of the article until its record is confirmed; the
long form lacks a report number for Duncan et al.\ (2001) and does not yet cite Lambert
(1993). The long-form bibliography is alphabetical by first author.

\point{R2.11 (minor).}{Affiliations and corresponding author.}
\reply Both article files carry the full affiliation block and the corresponding
author's email.

\point{R2.12 (minor).}{Notation.}
\reply Fixed as requested, with further changes after the second round: the
Clopper--Pearson bound is $\overline{R}(Y,B;\lambda)$, so $U$ is only the population,
$K_v$ only the key and $\gamma$ only a stopping time; $\alpha$ is defined where first
used; the abstract says ``at least its tolerance''.

\point{R2.13 (minor).}{Unclear table entries.}
\reply ``Unique share'' is dropped with its table. The $k=5$ pooled figure is explained
as Monte Carlo variation of 58 in 4,000 targets that reverses on a second draw. The
decision illustration uses the upper bound.

\point{R2.14 (minor).}{Monte Carlo standard errors in the toy audit.}
\reply Added. The overall false-certification rate is reported against $\alpha$ with its
standard error, and the adaptive-registration run is described as another estimate of
the single-coordinate rate, since the streams are independent.

\point{R2.15 (minor).}{Unlabelled and uncited figures.}
\reply The pooling figure is labelled and cited; the SENTRY-R diagram moved to the
supplement; the composition figure was dropped.

\point{R2.16 (minor).}{Undefined terms, puffery, meta-commentary.}
\reply SENTRY-R and LLM are defined at first use. The listed phrases are removed from
both manuscripts, together with the further stock phrases the second round found. The
network output is no longer called ``trust''; the quantity $1-\overline{R}_i/\tau_i$ is
the audited margin.

\point{R2.17 (minor).}{The two titles give no sign the papers are companions.}
\reply Both titles now refer to repeated releases or release programmes, and each
manuscript names the other.

\section*{Second round}
Reviewer~2's second-round check found ten further problems, all now fixed: the article
had merged the coarse and fine grids and read low audit power at $k=40$ as evidence of
excess risk; it claimed an advantage for adaptive registration that its experiment does
not test; ``about half and half'' omitted an interaction of 0.020; the long form called
noisy counts admissible while its own caption says they measure a different event; the
decision record lacked a caveat on selection across floors and named the wrong vintage;
a two-release pooled figure was compared only with the four-release bound (it is 2.8
times the bound of 0.148 for its own two releases); the self-citation gave no route to
the manuscript; Foster and Stine was cited for alpha-spending; and the superseded short
note is now marked as such in its source. Two reported problems were not errors: the
long-form bibliography is in order (entries with one first author sort by co-author),
and the toy audit's two runs of 10 in 2,000 use separate random draws.

\section*{Code and repository}
All 146 tests pass and \texttt{ruff} is clean. The main changes: the missing
\texttt{colab\_setup.py} was restored, so the test suite runs; \texttt{evaluation.py}
adds the fixed-readings attack, composed noisy counts and the bound-based choice;
\texttt{adaptive\_audit.py} and \texttt{sentry\_r.py} carry the fixes above;
\texttt{reporting.py} writes strict JSON and figures without creation dates, and two new
tables; \texttt{check\_paper\_numbers.py} has the stricter full mode and the new quoted
mode; \texttt{rebuild\_evidence.py} can publish tables to \texttt{paper/generated};
\texttt{.github/workflows} holds the CI and refresh workflows the README described. The
paper run in \texttt{artifacts-paper/} was regenerated; apart from the new results, every
number is unchanged except the transport-bound figures for the admissible count release
(0.004 and 0.027), which follow from composing the counts.
